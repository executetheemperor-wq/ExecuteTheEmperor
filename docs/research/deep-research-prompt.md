# Deep Research Prompt: Execute the Emperor

Paste everything below the line into a deep research chat (ChatGPT Deep Research, Claude Research, Gemini Deep Research, Perplexity, etc.).
Fill in the **Game Brief** first. The more specific it is, the more targeted the comparables will be. If a field is unknown, delete it rather than guessing.

---

## Role

You are a senior game design researcher preparing a research dossier for an indie team developing a game called **Execute the Emperor**. Your job is to find games that are equivalent or adjacent to it, dig up their **design documents, postmortems and lessons learned**, and turn that into findings we can act on, plus a curated **resource pack** we can work through ourselves.

## Game Brief (fill in)

- **One-line pitch:** [e.g. "A roguelike conspiracy game where you build a network inside the palace to assassinate the emperor before the purge finds you."]
- **Genre / sub-genres:** [ ]
- **Player fantasy:** [what the player should feel like they are]
- **Core loop:** [what the player does minute to minute, and run to run or session to session]
- **Perspective / format:** [video game (2D/3D, top-down, first-person, card-based, text-driven) or tabletop (board, card, social deduction, TTRPG)]
- **Single-player / multiplayer:** [ ]
- **Platform(s):** [ ]
- **Session length / campaign length:** [ ]
- **Setting and tone:** [historical, fantasy, sci-fi, satirical, grim...]
- **Team size / budget / timeline:** [ ]
- **Current stage:** [concept / prototype / vertical slice / production]
- **Our biggest open design questions:** [list 3-5, e.g. "How do we make planning feel as good as execution?", "How do we stop one dominant strategy?"]

If the brief is sparse, assume the game is about a person or faction plotting to overthrow, assassinate or execute a ruler, and cover all of the archetypes in the next section with equal weight.

## Research Scope

### 1. Find the comparable games

Identify 12-20 comparable games across these archetypes (skip any that clearly do not fit the brief, add any that do):

- **Assassination and stealth sandboxes:** planning and executing a kill on a high-value target (e.g. Hitman series, Dishonored, Thief, Assassin's Creed, Sniper Elite's kill-the-leader missions).
- **Political intrigue and court simulation:** influence, schemes, factions and succession (e.g. Crusader Kings II/III, King of Dragon Pass / Six Ages, Reigns, Suzerain, Yes, Your Grace, Sort the Court).
- **Conspiracy, heist and infiltration planning:** building a crew or plan before a single decisive attempt (e.g. Invisible, Inc., Monaco, Shadowrun Returns, Ocean's-style heist games, Blades in the Dark).
- **Revolution and resistance:** overthrowing a regime from below (e.g. Tropico's coups, Through the Darkest of Times, Frostpunk's dissent, Papers, Please, Liberté, Disco Elysium's politics).
- **Roguelikes and run-based games with a single ultimate target:** repeated attempts at an endboss or ruler (e.g. Hades, Slay the Spire, Inscryption, Darkest Dungeon, Loop Hero, Cultist Simulator).
- **Tabletop and social deduction:** hidden roles, betrayal, regicide (e.g. Coup, Secret Hitler, The Resistance / Avalon, Blood on the Clocktower, Battlestar Galactica, Root, Dune, A Game of Thrones: The Board Game, Twilight Imperium, Republic of Rome).
- **Narrative and choice-driven regicide or tyranny:** stories where killing or replacing the ruler is the climax (e.g. Dishonored's Empress plot, Vampire: Bloodlines' factions, The Banner Saga, 80 Days-style interactive fiction, Choice of Games titles).

For each, give a one-paragraph rationale: **what specifically about it maps to Execute the Emperor** (mechanic, structure, fantasy, tone, scope), and rate relevance as High / Medium / Low.

### 2. Dig up the design history (primary emphasis)

For each High-relevance game (aim for 8-12), find as many of these as exist:

- **Design documents:** original pitch docs, GDDs, design bibles, vertical-slice docs, level or mission design docs, puzzle docs, rulebook drafts. Prioritise publicly released originals (archive.org, developer sites, museum or university archives, book appendices, Kickstarter backer updates).
- **Postmortems:** Game Developer (formerly Gamasutra) postmortems, GDC talks (GDC Vault, including the free sections), "what went right / what went wrong" articles, developer blogs.
- **Developer commentary:** in-game commentary tracks, Noclip and other documentary interviews, podcast interviews (e.g. Designer Notes, Ludology, The Board Game Design Lab, Dinner with Designers), long-form press interviews, Reddit AMAs.
- **Dev diaries and iteration records:** Paradox dev diaries, Klei forum posts, Steam early access patch notes that show a design changing, BoardGameGeek designer diaries, playtest reports.
- **Critical and academic analysis:** DiGRA and FDG papers, game studies books, quality design-analysis essays and video essays (cite the author).

For each game, summarise:

1. **Original intent:** what the designers set out to make, and how the earliest documents describe it.
2. **What changed and why:** cut features, pivots, systems that were reworked after playtesting. Quote the source where possible.
3. **Stated lessons learned:** in the developers' own words, with citation.
4. **Problems they never solved:** criticisms from players and reviewers that the developers acknowledged, or that persisted.
5. **The transferable lesson for us:** one or two sentences, specific to Execute the Emperor.

### 3. Cross-cutting design themes

Synthesise across all the games. For each theme, state the recurring lessons, the patterns that worked, the anti-patterns, and the games that show each:

- **The single decisive target:** making one kill or climax feel earned, climactic and replayable rather than anticlimactic.
- **Planning vs. execution:** balancing preparation, information-gathering and improvisation when the plan breaks.
- **Information and secrecy:** fog of war, hidden information, suspicion and paranoia systems, detection and alert states.
- **Factions, loyalty and betrayal:** modelling NPC or player allegiance, bribery, blackmail, double agents.
- **Consequence and failure:** permadeath, run-based failure, partial success, branching outcomes, and how failure teaches without frustrating.
- **Emergence vs. authorship:** systemic sandboxes vs. scripted set pieces, and how each team managed scope.
- **Moral framing:** how games handle the ethics of assassination, tyranny and revolution without preaching or trivialising.
- **Scope and production:** how small and mid-size teams scoped systemic or intrigue-heavy games, what blew up schedules, and what they would cut next time.

### 4. Implications for Execute the Emperor

- The **top 10 actionable recommendations**, each tied to the evidence above and to our brief.
- **Five risks** the comparable games suggest we are likely to hit, with early warning signs and mitigations.
- **Three to five prototype experiments** we could run in a week or less to test our biggest open questions.
- **Open questions** the research could not answer.

## Resource Pack (required deliverable)

End with a curated resource pack for the team to investigate. Format it as a table, grouped into tiers:

- **Tier 1: Start here** (8-12 items, the highest-value primary sources)
- **Tier 2: Go deeper** (10-20 items)
- **Tier 3: Reference and rabbit holes** (anything else worth bookmarking)

Columns:

| Title | Game(s) | Type | Primary / Secondary | Link | Why it matters to us | What to look for | Time to consume |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

- **Type** is one of: Design doc, Postmortem, GDC talk, Dev diary, Interview / podcast, Documentary, Book, Paper, Video essay, Rulebook, Playable (game or prototype to play for study).
- **What to look for** names the specific section, timestamp, page or question to bring to it.
- Also include a short **"Play list"**: the 5-8 games (or specific missions, levels or scenarios) the team should actually play for study, with what to pay attention to in each and the approximate hours needed.
- Also include **"People to follow"**: designers, writers and researchers whose ongoing work is relevant, with where they publish.

## Source Rules

- **Cite everything.** Every claim about a game's design history needs a source link. Use direct quotes for developer statements.
- **Prefer primary sources** (the developers' own documents, talks and writing) over secondary summaries, and label which is which.
- **Never invent documents, quotes, talks or URLs.** If you believe a design doc exists but cannot find it, say so and list it under "leads to chase" with where it might be found.
- If a link is dead, provide an archive.org (Wayback Machine) link, or flag it as dead.
- Note the date of each source, and flag where a lesson may be outdated (e.g. pre-2015 distribution or tooling advice).
- Flag paywalled sources (e.g. parts of GDC Vault, books) and suggest free alternatives where they exist.
- When sources disagree, present both views.

## Output Structure

1. Executive summary (one page max: the five most important things we should know)
2. Comparable games matrix (game, archetype, year, team size if known, relevance rating, key mapping to our game)
3. Game deep dives (High-relevance games, using the five-part summary above)
4. Cross-cutting design themes
5. Implications for Execute the Emperor
6. Resource pack (tiers, play list, people to follow, leads to chase)
