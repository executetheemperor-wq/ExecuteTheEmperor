# The Emperor

The Emperor is **the objective** and the main source of the game's
interactions. Every decision should help either **your own position** or
**the assassination** — and those two should usually pull against each
other. *(OneNote › 4. The Emperor)*

## Design rule: tension

Early on, most things help. From the midgame on, it should be **rare for any
big decision to be purely good** — hiring a co-conspirator shouldn't both
raise your standing *and* get you closer to the Emperor for free. Good play
is rewarded through the systems you've built up, not through a card that's
simply strong. *(OneNote › 4. The Emperor)*

## Learning about him

Over a run you build up **intel**: where he walks, his routine, his
favourite drinks, the company he keeps. *(OneNote › 3.3 Co-Conspirator
Areas)* Political events add entries to the **Emperor Sheet**.
*(OneNote › Political Phase)*

Emperor?"** *(OneNote › 4. The Emperor)*

## Intel cards: his weaknesses

Each Emperor has weaknesses, shown as **intel** on a screen of cards — the
picture you build up of him before each assassination:

| Card | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Creature of Habit** | Keeps a strict routine — always in certain places at certain times. |
| **Partially Blind** | Blind in one eye, so he has a blind spot. |
| **Take My Wife, Please** | Trusts women above everyone else. |

Intel guides both your **political** and your **murder** plans, and decides
which options you have when planning the kill. *(OneNote › The night of the
murder)*

## His stats

He has **many of the same variables as co-conspirators**, plus special ones
for how much he notices you and how he stands with the people.
*(OneNote › 1. Variables)* Two lists exist (see
[Stats and checks](03-stats-and-checks.md#the-emperor)). The ones unique to
him:

- **Notice** — how much he's paying attention to *you*.
- **Senate** and **Public** relationship — his standing.
- **Traits & Ailments**.

*(OneNote › 1. Variables, Stats)*

## Steering him

Rather than reshaping your team to fit his habits, you can try to **plant
ideas in his head** and lead him somewhere that suits your plan.
*(OneNote › 3.3)* On the night, if the plot fails, the chance you're executed depends on his
**trust in you**, what you exploited, and whether he thinks you're behind
it. If you get out in a way that implicates someone else, he may grow
**closer** to you. *(OneNote › The night of the murder)*
