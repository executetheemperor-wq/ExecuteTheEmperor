# Board game

**Board game first** — decided 2026-10-11 ([D2](../decisions.md)).
*(Chat › Notes 2–4)* [Sprint 1](../sprint-1.md) is about finding the
30-second turn on paper.

- **Pressure tokens build up.**
- **Once they hit a value, you have to plot.**
- **Things are unlocked as you go.**

## How it lines up with the video game

*My reading:* it shares a backbone with the video game's [Pressure](02-pressure.md):

| Board game (Chat › Note 2) | Video game (OneNote › 1.1 Pressure) |
|---|---|
| Pressure tokens build | Pressure rises +1 a turn, plus cards, events, co-conspirators |
| At a value, you have to plot | At 100, the assassination runs on autopilot |
| Things unlock as you go | *Not said in the notes.* Could match the draft pool opening up as stats and Pressure change *(OneNote › 3. Co-Conspirators)*, or between-run upgrades *(OneNote › Between Runs)*. See [audit CA-67](../audit/contradictions.md#ca-67--board-game-things-unlocked-as-you-go-vs-the-digital-games-two-kinds-of-unlocks). |

**Conflict —** on the board, hitting a value *forces* you to plot. In the
video game you choose when to strike, and only 100 takes it out of your
hands. The notes don't give the board-game value, or say whether "plot"
means start planning or strike now. See [audit CA-43](../audit/contradictions.md#ca-43--choosing-when-to-strike-vs-being-forced-to-at-a-threshold-board-game-vs-digital).

*My take:* several other systems already suit the table — **roll-under
checks** (perhaps with a smaller die; see
[Sprint 1](../sprint-1.md#table-friendly-tweaks-suggestions)),
**co-conspirators as cards**, **intel cards**, and the **chessboard-style
area plan** (though the Emperor's route-finding may need simplifying).

## From the research

*From the [comparable-games research](../research/2026-10-12-comparable-games.md) (2026-10-12). Suggestions, not
decisions.*

- **Solo first, co-op second, hidden traitors last.** *Dune: Imperium* was
  built for 3–4 players and needed an automated rival for solo. *Heat*
  redesigned its bots because "players didn't understand how to run them".
  An AI rival conspirator is a big job of its own. Of the multiplayer
  options, co-op (a shared plot, maybe with a traitor later) fits "your
  allies are your problem" better than head-to-head.
  *([3.5](../research/2026-10-12-comparable-games.md#35-dune-imperium), [3.3](../research/2026-10-12-comparable-games.md#33-heat-pedal-to-the-metal),
  [4](../research/2026-10-12-comparable-games.md#4-cross-cutting-design-themes))*
- **A short solo game against one antagonist sells.** *Hostage Negotiator*
  is 15–30 minutes, solo, against one abductor. Its designer: "It is a
  fast-playing, very thematic, and – most of all – fun game. I've learned
  that those things trump old assumptions about things like player count."
  *([3.11](../research/2026-10-12-comparable-games.md#311-hostage-negotiator-and-career))*
- **Watch the rules length.** An early warning that systems are piling up:
  more than 4 pages of rules before 10 playtests.
  *([6](../research/2026-10-12-comparable-games.md#6-implications-for-execute-the-emperor))*

## Gaps

- Player count — solo, competitive (rival conspirators?), co-op?
- What builds pressure at the table.
- What "you have to plot" means as a rule.
- What gets unlocked, and when.
