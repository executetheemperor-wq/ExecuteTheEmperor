# Run structure

A **run** is one plot against one Emperor. In the early drafts that's the
whole game; later, runs chain into a succession of Emperors (see
[Between runs](09-between-runs.md)). *(OneNote › 1.1 Pressure)*

## The phases

The notes describe the phases twice, in slightly different shapes:

| "Basics" page (3 phases) | Phase pages (4) | What happens | Centrepiece |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 · Finding co-conspirators** | **Political Phase** | Learn about the Emperor, the co-conspirators and the state of politics; random events; build your own career. | — |
| | **Conspiracy Phase** | Meet the co-conspirators; steer the pact's decisions; set up the room to shape how you're regarded. | The forum — "shadowy circle" |
| **2 · Planning** | **Plotting Phase** *(empty page)* | Choose how and where; fund it; rig the palace — lock doors, swap guards. | Map of the Imperial Palace |
| **3 · The murder** | **The night of the murder** | The plan plays out; you're there or you're not. | Reports |

*(OneNote › Basics, Political Phase, Conspiracy Phase, Plotting Phase,
The night of the murder)*

**Conflict —** three phases or four? A natural reading is that Political and
Conspiracy are the public and secret halves of phase 1, but the notes never
say so. See [audit](../audit.md).

## Moving through a run: the map

A **Slay the Spire-style path** into events: you choose which events come
next, with only "slight foresight" rather than a full map — foresight that
co-conspirators or events can improve. *(OneNote › Basics, 5. The Gameplay
Loop)* More in [Cards, events and pathing](05-cards-events-and-pathing.md).

## Turns and the clock

- **Every turn adds 1 Pressure.** *(OneNote › 1.1 Pressure)*
- You choose **when** to launch the assassination; Pressure is what stops
  you waiting forever. *(OneNote › 1.1 Pressure)*

**Gap —** what a "turn" is: one node on the map? one event? one phase?

## How a run ends

| Ending | What the notes say |
|---|---|
| **The Emperor dies** | Whether you get away with it, and what your position is afterwards, depend on how you set up the scene. *(The night of the murder)* |
| **The plot is foiled** | A chance you're executed — based on the Emperor's trust in you, what you exploited, and whether they think you're behind it. Or you slip out and implicate someone else, and the Emperor grows *closer* to you. *(The night of the murder)* |
| **Someone rats you out** | If a co-conspirator you left alive betrays you, there's a chance you're ended. *(Basics)* |
| **Pressure hits 100** | "More or less a lose condition": the assassination runs on autopilot — someone will make a move. You *can* still win, but the odds are against you. *(1.1 Pressure)* |

When a plot fails, the book closes and the [between-runs](09-between-runs.md)
sequence begins. *(OneNote › Between Runs)*

## The long game

"Then restart. Need to kill certain number of emporers to be crowned
yourself." *(Basics)* Later drafts: each assassination puts a new Emperor
on the throne — unless your stats and favour are high enough that it's you —
and the succession knocks Pressure down, though not to zero.
*(OneNote › 1.1 Pressure)*

## Gaps

- What a turn is, and how many turns a run lasts.
- How the phases hand over to each other — fixed order, or does the map mix
  political and conspiracy events?
- How a run *starts*: who you are, what you begin with (the Player Character
  page is empty).
- Whether "restart" follows only failure, or success too.
