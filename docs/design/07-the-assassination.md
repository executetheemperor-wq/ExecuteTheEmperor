## The area plan: a chessboard

Set the scene "as if chess pieces are set up on a board, a recreation of the
event on paper". Depending on your **influence**, you choose:

- which **doors to lock**
- where to put **spies**
- who to **bribe** — and which **guards to replace** with your own people
  *(Basics)*
- where your **conspirators** stand

These change **the Emperor's route**, **how many guards** he has and **whose
side they're on**. The aim: steer him into the kill zone, away from his
guards, or into a trap. *(OneNote › The night of the murder)*

## The palace map

Parts of the palace map are **obscured** — building works, or because you
don't have the plans. The **closer you get to the Emperor**, the more you
can do in planning. *(OneNote › Basics)* A between-runs upgrade lets you
bribe architects and workers for the plans — and place a free spy in any
room. *(OneNote › Between Runs)*

## Be there, or don't

| | You're there | You're not |
|---|---|---|
| **What you get** | You see exactly how people act; you learn more and gain more tricks. | You rely on word of mouth and reports. |
| **What it costs** | You're more likely to be implicated, or a target of suspicion. | Plots may be growing or not — it depends on how you read people's reports. |

Sometimes you **can't** be there at all. *(OneNote › Basics, The night of
the murder)*

The original idea gave phase 3 its own centrepiece: **Reports**.
*(OneNote › Basics)*

## Outcomes

- **Success:** the set-up decides whether you get away with it and what
  your position is afterwards.
- **Foiled:** a chance you're executed, based on the Emperor's trust in you,
  what you exploited, and whether he thinks you're behind it. Get out in a
  way that implicates someone else and he may grow closer to you.
- **Pressure at 100:** the night runs on autopilot and you can only watch.
  *(1.1 Pressure)*
