# Between runs and the crown

## When a plot fails: the book

The failed run turns out to be **a story**. A book closes over the whole
screen, and you see it's being **read to a young, impressionable man**.

He chooses **one of three lessons** he's taken from it. The lessons come
from *why* the run failed — for example:

- no oversight on the palace map;
- too much suspicion gained in the political phase;
- betrayal by enemies.

Each lesson gives a **lasting, permanent upgrade** and a **stat boost** in
that area for the next run. *(OneNote › Between Runs)*

### Example

> **No oversight on the palace grounds** → the ability to **bribe architects
> and workers** for the plans → place a **free personal spy** in any room to
> report back on how it went.

### Upgrade caps

Each area can take **up to *x* permanent upgrades** — like Darkest Dungeon
II's candles of hope. *(OneNote › Between Runs)*

## The crown

The long-term goal: **kill a certain number of Emperors and be crowned
yourself.** *(Basics)*

- **Early drafts:** one assassination per game.
- **Later drafts:** many assassinations. After each, **a new Emperor** takes
  the throne — unless your stats and favour mean it's **you**. A succession
  also **reduces Pressure** (partly).

*(OneNote › 1.1 Pressure)*

## Gaps

- **Who is the young man?** The next conspirator? The player's heir? The
  same page later calls him "the child" — which is it?
- **What happens after a *successful* run** — does the book only appear on
  failure?
- **How many Emperors** to win, and does the run continue straight into the
  next one?
- **The value of *x*** and the full list of upgrade areas.
- **What carries over** between Emperors within one game (co-conspirators,
  Pressure, intel, stats).
