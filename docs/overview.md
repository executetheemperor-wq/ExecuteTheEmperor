## The pitch

You're an ambitious figure in Imperial Rome, and the Emperor has to die.

You never swing the knife in an action sequence. *Execute the Emperor* is a
**resource-management deckbuilder**: you gather gold, favour and access;
recruit co-conspirators, who come into your hand as cards; learn the
Emperor's habits and weaknesses; and lay out the murder like pieces on a
chessboard. Then, on the night, your set-up — played out with dice, stats
and cards — decides whether it works and whether anyone works out it was
you. (Exactly how it resolves is still open.)

The aim of a run is to **kill the Emperor without being suspected**. The
longer aim is to kill enough Emperors that the crown ends up on **your**
head. *(OneNote › 1. Variables, Basics, The night of the murder)*

## Genre, in your words

- "A resource management game primarily… a deckbuilder at heart, using
  cards to impart information or determine player actions, and will also
  involve some level of pathing." *(OneNote › 1. Variables)*
- "More deckbuilder/management than actual murder, so dice rolls, stats
  cards etc." *(OneNote › The night of the murder)*
- *My label:* a **roguelite** structure — runs end, a framing story carries
  lessons forward, and permanent upgrades accrue. *(OneNote › Between Runs)*

| **Slay the Spire** | Map pathing into events; card pools; negative "curse" cards; showing lots of information about results. |
| **Darkest Dungeon II** | Candles of hope → capped permanent upgrades between runs. |

## Design principles (pulled from across the notes)

1. **Decisions in tension.** From the midgame on, almost no choice should be
   purely good — help your standing *or* the plot, rarely both for free.
   *(OneNote › 4. The Emperor)*
2. **Rewards come from systems, not from "this card is good".** Playing well
   is rewarded through interactions the player has built up.
   *(OneNote › 4. The Emperor)*
3. **Information is the real currency.** Intel about the Emperor decides
   which plans are even possible; being at the murder buys knowledge at the
   price of suspicion. *(OneNote › The night of the murder, 3.3)*
4. **A clock you can't stop.** Pressure is easy to gain, very hard to
   shed, and turns every run into a snowball. *(OneNote › 1.1 Pressure)*
5. **Your allies are your problem.** Co-conspirators are sticky; you play
   around their bad interactions rather than trimming them away.
   *(OneNote › 3.4 Removing Co-Conspirators)*
6. **Show the maths.** Like Spire, show the player a lot about the results
   of boosts and stats. *(OneNote › The night of the murder)*
