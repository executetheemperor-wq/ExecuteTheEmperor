## Missing mechanics, by doc

### Overview

Doc: [Overview](../overview.md)

#### OV-01 · Who the player character is — 🟠 important

**In the notes:** OneNote › 2. The Player Character (empty page). OneNote › Between Runs: the book is 'being read to a young, impressionable man'. OneNote › The night of the murder: you may be 'present'. OneNote › Political Phase: 'your own political career'.

**Why it matters:** Undefined: who you are (senator, courtier, general?), what position you start in (this sets your access and 'closeness'), and whether the young man becomes the next PC. Matters for theme and starting stats. A prototype can pick one.

#### OV-02 · What the player can see vs what is hidden or false — 🟠 important

**In the notes:** OneNote › The night of the murder: 'We want to have, similarly to spire, a lot of information shown about the results of these boosts, stats etc.' OneNote › 1. Variables: 'Traits of certain co-conspirators should be revealed to the player', while a happy socialite 'may spin yarns or lie to you about certain effects'. OneNote › Basics: palace 'parts can be obscured'; reports depend 'on how you interpret some people's reports'.

**Why it matters:** The notes want Slay-the-Spire-style transparency and also hidden traits, lies, obscured maps and unreliable reports. They never say which numbers you see exactly, which are hidden or fuzzy, or how a lie is shown. That decides how every card and sheet is laid out.

### Run structure

Doc: [Run structure](../design/01-run-structure.md)

#### RS-01 · What a 'turn' (and a 'round') is — 🔴 blocker

**In the notes:** OneNote › 1.1 Pressure: 'each turn should add 1 pressure, such that the clock ticks up as the game progresses'. OneNote › 3. Co-Conspirators: pool 'will persist between rounds'. OneNote › Bonus events ('Plotting on the Ides') and OneNote › The night of the murder ('can always be found doing certain things at certain times') imply a calendar or time of day. No page defines a turn, a round, or in-game time; OneNote › 5. The Gameplay Loop covers only pathing.

**Why it matters:** Pressure is the run clock and the main lose condition, and it ticks per turn. Without knowing what a turn is (a map node, an action, a day), the '+1 per turn' and '+5 for a speech' numbers can't be paced. Run length can't be estimated either.

#### RS-02 · Which phases a run has, in what order, and where each ends — 🔴 blocker

**In the notes:** OneNote › Basics / Chat › Note 1 list three phases: 'finding co-conspirators', 'planning', 'the murder', with 'Forum shadowy circle... first phase', 'Map of imperial palace in second phase', 'Reports in third phase'. The same OneNote section also has separate pages 'Political Phase', 'Conspiracy Phase', 'Plotting Phase' (empty) and 'The night of the murder'. OneNote › Between Runs refers to 'the political phase'.

**Why it matters:** The notes give three phases in one place and four in another. Never stated: whether Political comes before or after Conspiracy, whether they alternate on the event path or are blocks, and whether 'planning' and the Plotting Phase are the same. The order decides when you draft, when you gather intel and when you place pieces.

#### RS-03 · Pathing map structure — 🟠 important

**In the notes:** OneNote › Basics: 'Slay the Spire esque pathing system, path into events'. OneNote › 5. The Gameplay Loop: 'There shouldn't be a full path, but instead some slight foresight, maybe improved by co-conspirators or events'.

**Why it matters:** Undefined: node types, map length, how many nodes ahead you see, how co-conspirators or events improve foresight, and whether meetings and drafts are nodes. A prototype can stand in a shuffled event deck. The real game needs the structure because it sets pacing and how much the player can plan around Pressure.

#### RS-04 · Launching the assassination: the trigger and what you need — 🔴 blocker

**In the notes:** OneNote › 1.1 Pressure: 'players will have the freedom to choose when to go ahead with the assassination'; 'The player will want to choose to begin the assassination well before that point'. OneNote › The night of the murder: 'We need to decide a few things - the method, the area, the plan'. OneNote › 3.3: 'You need a co-conspirator who has access to the area of the playhouse'.

**Why it matters:** Undefined: what action launches the plot, what minimum you need (area access, a method, any conspirators), whether a Plotting step comes between the decision and the night, and whether you can back out. This is the player's main strategic decision against the Pressure clock.

#### RS-05 · Starting state of a run — 🔴 blocker

**In the notes:** OneNote › 1. Variables: variables 'should be distributed at average being around the 50 mark'. OneNote › 3. Co-Conspirators: 'you may have 3 initial co-conspirators available to draft'. Nothing on starting Pressure, money, cards or party.

**Why it matters:** A first turn can't be played without starting values for stats, money and Pressure, the starting card pool, and whether you begin with any conspirator.

#### RS-06 · Win and lose conditions for a single run — 🔴 blocker

**In the notes:** OneNote › 1. Variables: 'gain the gold, favour and access needed to kill the emperor without being suspected'; Pressure 'is an important win or lose condition'. OneNote › 1.1 Pressure: at 100, 'more or less a lose condition'. OneNote › Between Runs: 'When a plot is failed', with causes such as 'too much suspicion gained in the political phase, or betrayal of enemies'. OneNote › The night of the murder: 'determine if you get away with it, and what your position is after the murder'.

**Why it matters:** Not stated whether a run is won by killing the emperor, by killing him unsuspected, or by reaching a good 'position after'. Also missing: which events end a run before the night (suspicion, betrayal), and how Pressure counts as a 'win' condition at all. Without these a prototype has no end state.

### Pressure

Doc: [Pressure](../design/02-pressure.md)

#### PR-01 · Pressure thresholds: start value and what each band switches on — 🔴 blocker

**In the notes:** OneNote › 1.1 Pressure: 'Increased pressure allows for better co-conspirators to be drafted'; 'as pressure gets higher, certain co-conspirators may force their way into the group, blackmailing you'; 'low pressure means fewer opportunities'; 'Once pressure hits ~80... snowball into 100 very soon'. OneNote › 3. Co-Conspirators: better conspirators 'the greater the pressure, up to a point. Pressure that is too great may cause unstable or unreliable co-conspirators to spawn'.

**Why it matters:** Pressure is 'the most important global variable', but only ~80 and 100 are numbers. Missing: the starting value, what counts as 'low' and 'too great', where each carrot and stick effect starts, and what mechanically makes ~80 snowball.

#### PR-02 · Pressure magnitudes, sources and the 'handful' of reducers — 🟠 important

**In the notes:** OneNote › 1.1 Pressure: 'each turn should add 1 pressure'; 'Certain cards or events will add pressure by chance'; a denouncing speech 'may increment the pressure by 5'; 'Co-conspirators may also bring passive pressure gain'; 'no more than a handful of effects that can stall or reduce pressure'; surviving 100 needs 'very certain circumstances'; a new emperor is 'one mechanism for the pressure to reduce (but not fully)'.

**Why it matters:** Only two magnitudes are given (+1 per turn, +5 for a speech). The reducers, the 'very certain circumstances' and the size of passive gains are unnamed, and they decide whether the clock feels fair or arbitrary.

### Stats and checks

Doc: [Stats and checks](../design/03-stats-and-checks.md)

#### ST-01 · No canonical stat list: three lists disagree — 🔴 blocker

**In the notes:** OneNote › Stats, PC: 'Honorific, Military, Political, Economical, Personal, Virtue, Age, Health, Public Support' ('Personal' is both a heading and an item). OneNote › 1. Variables, player: 'Money, Influence, Charm, Military, Political'. Co-conspirator: 'Happiness, Suspicion, Gullibility' vs OneNote › Stats Interpersonal 'Likeability, Gullibility, Trust, Suspicion'. Emperor: OneNote › Stats list vs OneNote › 1. Variables 'Notice, Influence, Money, Happiness, Suspicion, Military, Senate Relationship, Public relationship'.

**Why it matters:** Charm and Influence drive drafting and the d100 example, yet the Stats page lacks both. Happiness and Likeability may be the same thing. Every card, cost, event and check names a stat, so the list has to be fixed before anything can be written.

#### ST-02 · Global vs local variables: the definition goes in a circle — 🟠 important

**In the notes:** OneNote › 1. Variables: 'Global being variables that apply to the player, and can be contributed to by any source, and local variables being those that apply to the player, or to NPCs, and can be contributed to by only the player or NPCs'. OneNote › Stats scopes stats differently: Public Relations '(Apply to Rome as a Whole)', Senate Relations '(Apply to the Senate as a Whole)', Interpersonal '(Apply to other NPCs)'.

**Why it matters:** By the definition, both kinds 'apply to the player'. The only difference given, who may contribute, has no clear in-play meaning. That leaves the storage of Senate and Public relations open: one global number or one per NPC.

#### ST-03 · How stat correlations and conspirator traits actually work — 🟠 important

**In the notes:** OneNote › 1. Variables: 'increased happiness should decrease suspicion generally'; 'your money should positively correlate with influence in every run'; 'a socialite co-conspirator may positively correlate with your charm, but negatively correlate with your militarism'; 'your charm may inversely effect their happiness'.

**Why it matters:** This is the main 'balance how you grow' tension, but the mechanism is unstated. A correlation could be a fixed link (every +N to X moves Y), a drift each turn, or a modifier applied when a stat changes. Magnitudes and the number of traits per conspirator are also missing.

#### ST-04 · When d100 checks happen and how cards change them — 🔴 blocker

**In the notes:** OneNote › 3.1: 'an influence stat of 70 means that a d100 is rolled, and if your score is smaller than or equal to your influence, you pass this check'; 'creating greater opportunities for influence rolls'. OneNote › The night of the murder: 'dice rolls, stats cards etc.' OneNote › 3.2: 'an event may require a military card to be played'.

**Why it matters:** Two resolution systems are named, roll-under d100 and playing a tagged card, and their relationship is never given. Does a card add to the target number, replace the roll, or just unlock the option? 'Greater opportunities' could mean more checks or better odds. This is the core action-resolution rule.

#### ST-05 · Suspicion: which value counts and how it gets you caught — 🔴 blocker

**In the notes:** Suspicion appears under Public Relations, Senate Relations and Interpersonal in OneNote › Stats, and for co-conspirators and the Emperor in OneNote › 1. Variables. OneNote › 1. Variables goal: 'without being suspected'. OneNote › Between Runs failure cause: 'too much suspicion gained in the political phase'. OneNote › The night of the murder: execution chance 'based upon the emperors trust in you', though Trust is in neither Emperor stat list.

**Why it matters:** There are at least five suspicion values and no rule for which one gets you caught, at what threshold, or whether that can happen mid-run. Between Runs says it can end a run in the political phase. Being caught is the main stick besides Pressure.

#### ST-06 · Money/gold: stat or currency, where it comes from, what it costs — 🔴 blocker

**In the notes:** OneNote › 1. Variables: 'gain the gold, favour and access'; Money is a player and Emperor variable, and 'All variables... will be ranked out of 100'. Sources: OneNote › Political Phase events 'can give you money or other boons'; OneNote › Basics 'you can acquire funding to do it in a certain way'. Sinks: OneNote › Propaganda Campaigns 'they cost gold'; OneNote › 3. 'Some may cost money', blackmail 'spending money and pressure'; OneNote › 3.4 'paying off (at cost to the player's funds)'; OneNote › The night of the murder 'who to bribe'. OneNote › Stats has 'Economical' instead of Money.

**Why it matters:** Undefined: whether money is a 0–100 stat or a currency you spend (spending from a stat would also lower your d100 odds), whether money, gold, funding and 'Economical' are one thing, income beyond random events, and any prices. Many costs are paid in it.

#### ST-07 · Stats with no stated meaning — ⚪ later

**In the notes:** OneNote › Stats lists 'Honorific' as a stat, while OneNote › Honorifics describes titles such as being 'known as a cheater'. It also lists 'Personal' twice, 'Virtue' (PC, Public, Senate), and 'Age'/'Health' for both PC and Emperor. OneNote › 1. Variables defines Gullibility as 'how much they think they can get away with lying to you', then says a socialite 'may also find you more gullible'.

**Why it matters:** Each needs a meaning and a use, or a cut. For example, can Age or Health end an emperor or the PC naturally? Low impact for a first prototype.

### Co-conspirators

Doc: [Co-conspirators](../design/04-co-conspirators.md)

#### CC-01 · Co-conspirator card template — 🔴 blocker

**In the notes:** OneNote › 3. Co-Conspirators: 'a type of card that both improve certain stats, unlock new areas and abilities, allow for further in-game events and interactions'. OneNote › 3.1: 'Each one will come with their own standing and variables, and these will increment onto your adjusted base variables'. OneNote › 3.2: 'each coming with their own cards'. OneNote › 3.3: access to areas and people. OneNote › 1. Variables: traits and per-conspirator Happiness/Suspicion/Gullibility.

**Why it matters:** The fields appear as ideas but never as a template. Missing: bonus sizes, cards per conspirator, the tag set for area/access/means, how many traits, their own stat values, and cost. This is the game's core content unit.

#### CC-02 · Draft procedure, timing and party size — 🔴 blocker

**In the notes:** OneNote › 3. Co-Conspirators: 'Each Co-Conspirator will need to be drafted from a selection'; 'you may have 3 initial co-conspirators available to draft'; the pool 'will persist between rounds'. OneNote › 1. Variables: 'the player eventually is forced to draft a wide pool of co-conspirators'. OneNote › Basics / Chat › Note 1: 'Hiring them or charming them or blackmailing them'.

**Why it matters:** Undefined: when a draft happens (a map node, the conspiracy meeting, any turn), how many you take, the party cap and minimum, what 'forced to draft a wide pool' forces, and how the pool refreshes. Party size sets the deck and how many people's happiness you manage.

#### CC-03 · Availability floors and ceilings, and pool changes — 🟠 important

**In the notes:** OneNote › 3. Co-Conspirators: 'One that is military focused may require a military floor to trigger, a better military man may need a higher floor, but if you surpass that floor then they may no longer be available'; 'progression may cause a possible co-conspirator to lose interest'.

**Why it matters:** A floor plus a cap ('surpass' reads as a ceiling) makes each recruit a window to aim for, but there are no numbers and no rule for how the player sees conspirators enter and leave the pool. Needed for stat growth to drive drafting.

#### CC-04 · Recruitment costs: hire, charm, pay, blackmail and forced joins — 🟠 important

**In the notes:** OneNote › 3. Co-Conspirators: the cost is 'either a minimum requirement, or costing a reduction in a variable stat'; 'you can choose to pay influence to hire them' (the lout/womaniser); 'some may join without your consent, especially as the pressure rises'; blackmail is 'spending money and pressure... at a cost of their reduced happiness, greater suspicion, and mounting pressure'. OneNote › 1.1 Pressure: forced joiners 'may want money, or charm, or force cards into your hand'.

**Why it matters:** Hire, charm, pay and blackmail are named but not told apart. Missing: what each costs, what each sets (starting Happiness and Suspicion), and how forced joins are triggered.

#### CC-05 · 'Hate' and 'grasp' are not stats anywhere — 🟠 important

**In the notes:** OneNote › Basics / Chat › Note 1: 'Different values of like hate, the more you have them in your grasp the more they either need to be part of it or not'. Neither hate nor grasp appears in OneNote › Stats or OneNote › 1. Variables, which use Happiness, Likeability and Trust.

**Why it matters:** Need to decide whether grasp is a separate leverage stat (fed by blackmail?) or folds into Happiness and Suspicion, and whether hate is a stat at all. This affects recruitment and betrayal.

#### CC-06 · What moves conspirator happiness, and when they betray you — 🔴 blocker

**In the notes:** OneNote › 3.2: 'Co-conspirators become happier when you use their cards'; 'The more you refuse to use conspirator cards, the more unhappy they get. Overusing one conspirator's card may also cause other conspirators to become suspicious of you'. OneNote › Basics / Chat › Note 1: 'if they rat you out you have a chance of being ended'. OneNote › 1.1 Pressure: 'else they'll spill the beans'. OneNote › Between Runs: failure through 'betrayal of enemies'.

**Why it matters:** Happiness is the core management loop and betrayal is its stick, but neither is defined. Missing: what counts as 'refusing' (drawn but not played?), how much happiness moves, what triggers a rat-out (a threshold or a d100?), and what it does (ends the run, spikes suspicion, adds Pressure).

#### CC-07 · Relations between conspirators and what 'discord' does — 🟠 important

**In the notes:** OneNote › 3.3: 'if military men do not get along with the acting types, then you need to carefully manage your pivot, all the while pressure grows and discord is sown'. OneNote › 3.2: overusing one conspirator's card makes 'other conspirators... suspicious of you'.

**Why it matters:** Undefined whether conspirators relate to each other directly (pairwise likes and dislikes, factions) or only through your stats (the charm vs military link). 'Discord' has no effect. This drives the notes' 'pivot' dilemma.

#### CC-08 · Removing and killing conspirators: costs and limits — 🟠 important

**In the notes:** OneNote › 3.4: 'Only a select few events or cards should be allow the player to trim their co-conspirators'; some conspirators 'may have an ability to remove another, by paying off... by charming'; killing one is 'available at all points' but 'could reasonably only be done once ever in a run'. OneNote › Basics / Chat › Note 1: 'Can execute them or leave them alive'.

**Why it matters:** Missing: the costs ('great cost of suspicion from other co-conspirators, and worsened stats'), whether once per run is a hard limit, and whether executing a recruit who refuses (Basics) is the same action. A first prototype can leave this out.

#### CC-09 · What happens at the conspiracy meeting (the forum) — 🟠 important

**In the notes:** OneNote › Conspiracy Phase: 'Meeting with co-conspirators. Can influence decisions from the pact, set up the room to influence in what regard you're held, and use this to try to push people in certain directions'. OneNote › Basics / Chat › Note 1: 'Forum shadowy circle, where discuss the plot in first phase'.

**Why it matters:** Undefined: what the pact's 'decisions' are (votes on area or method?), what 'set up the room' means as a mechanic (seating? who is invited?), what 'push people' changes, and how often meetings happen. This is the conspiracy phase's only scene.

### Cards, events and pathing

Doc: [Cards, events and pathing](../design/05-cards-events-and-pathing.md)

#### CE-01 · Card template and card pool types — 🔴 blocker

**In the notes:** OneNote › 1. Variables: 'using cards to impart information or determine player actions'. OneNote › The night of the murder: 'dice rolls, stats cards etc.'

**Why it matters:** There's no card template (tag or pool, value, cost, effect) and no list of pools (one per stat?). Cards are named as the game's main interface.

#### CE-02 · A hand, or drawing from a pool at each event? And what can you add to your deck? — 🔴 blocker

**In the notes:** OneNote › 1.1 Pressure: blackmailers 'force cards into your hand' (implies a hand). OneNote › 3.2: 'Whenever an event or action occurs, cards will be drawn out of the pool that correlates to it' (implies per-event draws); 'you cannot add or remove cards corresponding to a co-conspirator, you may only add or remove co-conspirators'; 'the combined pool of yours and your co-conspirators'.

**Why it matters:** Undefined: whether you hold a hand between events, how many cards an event draws, whether you choose what to play, and where your own cards come from. Also whether your own cards can be added or removed; if not, drafting conspirators is the only deckbuilding action in a game 'a deckbuilder at heart'.

#### CE-03 · Event template and event list — 🔴 blocker

**In the notes:** OneNote › Events (empty page). OneNote › Political Phase: 'Different random events that add information to the Emperor Sheet, can give you money or other boons, and can be used to effect public opinion'. OneNote › 3.2: 'Other events may allow combinations of cards, which will be indicated in advance'. OneNote › Honorifics: an event 'by which you cheat at a game'. OneNote › 1.1: 'Certain cards or events will add pressure by chance'.

**Why it matters:** Events are what the map paths into, and none is specified. Missing: the structure (prompt, options, requirements, success and failure outcomes), how 'by chance' Pressure is rolled, and how conspirators 'allow for further in-game events'.

#### CE-04 · One-time cards: when you can play them — 🟠 important

**In the notes:** OneNote › 3.2: 'one time use cards, usable outside of events. One may allow for a propaganda campaign to be used, costing an amount of money and increasing pressure in return for boosting one of your stats'.

**Why it matters:** Undefined: when 'outside of events' is (between nodes? any time?), whether playing one costs a turn (+1 Pressure), and whether one-time cards still count towards a conspirator's happiness.

#### CE-05 · Negative cards, curses and forced cards — 🟠 important

**In the notes:** OneNote › 3.2: 'these can be positive or negative (think curses in slay the spire for example)'. OneNote › 1.1 Pressure: blackmailers 'force cards into your hand that are of less use to you'.

**Why it matters:** Undefined: what a negative card does when drawn (clogs the draw, forces a bad effect, adds Pressure?) and whether forced cards are permanent. Curses are what make a bad conspirator hurt.

#### CE-06 · The Ides and other bonus events — ⚪ later

**In the notes:** OneNote › Bonus events: 'Plotting on the Ides is harder but lessens suspicion - emporers are more prepared and on guard, but the omen makes it so less aspersions are put on the co-conspirator'.

**Why it matters:** Needs a calendar (see RS-01) and a point where you choose it. Does 'plotting' mean the night or the planning turns? How big are the effects, and what other bonus events exist (the page title is plural)? A flavour mechanic, safe to defer.

### The Emperor

#### EM-01 · How you gain intel on the Emperor — 🔴 blocker

#### AS-04 · Outcomes of the night: getting away with it, position after, foiled plots — 🔴 blocker

**Why it matters:** Needs an outcome table: success and escaped, success but implicated, foiled and executed, foiled and survived, foiled with someone else framed. Undefined: what 'position after' means (a step towards becoming emperor?), what 'what you exploit' refers to, and how you frame someone else.

#### AS-07 · Being there or not on the night — 🟠 important

**In the notes:** OneNote › The night of the murder: 'Choose whether you'll be present or not: if you're present you'll learn more and gain more tricks, but you will be more like to be implicated or a target of suspicion'. #### AS-08 · Reports, word of mouth and 'plots growing' — 🟠 important

#### AS-09 · The Pressure-100 'autopilot' assassination — 🟠 important

**Why it matters:** Undefined: who acts, with what area, method and plan (your half-built one?), how the odds are set, and whether the player has any say. A prototype can treat 100 as a loss, but the intended feel depends on this.

- SUGGESTION (not from the notes): this is the smallest set of decisions for a paper prototype of ONE assassination run, as the notes ask (OneNote › 1.1 Pressure: 'for earlier drafts of the game we should stick to one assassination'). Every default in [brackets] is my own placeholder, meant to make each decision quick to answer or override.
- SUGGESTION: Decide what one turn is and the shape of a run (RS-01, RS-02, RS-03). [Suggested default: 1 turn = 1 event node; a short branching path of ~15 nodes with 2 nodes visible ahead; Political and Conspiracy as node types on the same path rather than separate blocks; a single Plotting step after you launch, then the Night.]
- SUGGESTION: Fix a cut-down stat sheet (ST-01, ST-06). [Suggested default: player Money, Influence, Charm, Military, Political (the OneNote › 1. Variables list) plus one player Suspicion; conspirators track only Happiness; the Emperor tracks only Notice/Suspicion; all 0–100, starting near 50 as the notes say. Treat Money as a spendable 0–100 pool and never roll checks against it.]
- SUGGESTION: Fix the check rule and how cards change it (ST-04). [Suggested default: roll a d100 and pass at or under the relevant stat (OneNote › 3.1); each card played adds its value to the target number for that check.]
- SUGGESTION: Fix the Pressure numbers (PR-01, PR-02). [Suggested default: start at 10; +1 per turn (from the notes); bold cards add +3 to +5. Bands: under 30, conspirators cost more; 30–79, better draft offers and an occasional forced blackmailer; 80+, an extra +1 per turn. 100 = loss in the prototype, with the autopilot deferred. - SUGGESTION: Fix the card template and how cards are drawn (CE-01, CE-02). - SUGGESTION: Fix the event template and write ~15 events (CE-03, EM-01). [Suggested default: a prompt + 2–3 options; each option has a requirement (a card tag or a d100 check) and outcomes (stat changes, Money, Pressure, an intel card, or a new draft offer). At least 5 events reveal intel.]
- SUGGESTION: Fix the co-conspirator template and draft rule, and write ~8 conspirators (CC-01, CC-02). - SUGGESTION: Define one fixed Emperor (EM-02, EM-03). [Suggested default: a static stat line plus 4 hidden intel cards: the notes' 'Creature of Habit', 'Partially blind', 'Take my Wife, please' and the actors/plays/wine habit from OneNote › 3.3. - SUGGESTION: Fix 2–3 areas, 2–3 methods and what you need to launch (AS-01, RS-04). [Suggested default: Imperial Palace, Private Dinner and Gardens from OneNote › Areas; poison, blade and a bribed guard as methods. - SUGGESTION: Fix a simple area plan and Night resolution (AS-02, AS-03, AS-07).
